Bill one

The energy bill, led by the line no broker can touch.

Any broker can shop the price of a kilowatt hour. Nobody can shop the demand charge. It is set by the utility from the highest fifteen minutes of the month, and in a big box that is a summer afternoon when every rooftop unit is running flat out.

A unit that has lost a quarter of its capacity to oil fouling does not draw less. It runs longer and lands harder on that peak. Restore the heat transfer and two things happen: each compressor draws less power at the same load, and fewer units are stacked at the top of the afternoon. The demand line falls and, on ratcheted tariffs, the credit lasts twelve months.

The kilowatt hours fall with it. On units past their tenth year the recovery has measured 20 to 24 percent of cooling energy under IPMVP. That is the part of this page with the most evidence behind it.

Academy's 2021 GHG supplement puts purchased electricity at 94 percent of the company's measured footprint.

Bill two

The maintenance bill. Heat is what kills compressors.

Fouled coils raise head pressure and discharge temperature. That is the mechanism behind most compressor failures, most refrigerant loss, and most of the emergency calls that land in July. Refrigerant leakage already appears in Academy's own Scope 1 line.

Restoring heat transfer drops both readings within 48 hours of runtime, on every unit, and it is visible on a gauge. We estimate 15 to 25 percent fewer repair and failure events on an older roof. That is a claim until a service log proves it, which is exactly what the pilot is for.

The number a VP feels is not the invoice. It is the store that is hot on a Saturday in July, and what that does to sales that day.

Bill three

The replacement cycle. About $240,000 a roof, 322 roofs.

About twelve rooftop units per store at about $20,000 each installed. Median service life around fifteen years. Across the fleet that is roughly $77 million of replacement exposure, arriving on failure, in summer, one store at a time.

A unit that runs cooler and cycles less lasts longer. Three to five added years drops the annualized cost of the roof and, more usefully, moves replacement onto the twenty to twenty five stores a year Academy already builds and remodels, instead of onto the emergency line.

This is an engineering argument until a fleet proves it. The evidence it rests on is the same gauge reading from bill two, taken on every treated unit before and after.

Mechanism

Why one treatment moves all three.

Compressor oil migrates into the refrigerant and bakes onto the inside of the coil walls as an insulating film. Heat that should leave the building stays in the unit, so the compressor runs hotter and longer to do the same job. A one time treatment of the refrigerant circuit strips that film and keeps the metal clean, with no shutdown, no modification, and the equipment warranty in place.

The ask

Prove it in five stores. Measured on all three bills.

  1. 01

    Send the folder.

    Twelve months of bills with the demand line, the rooftop unit list with ages, and whatever maintenance spend you have per store. One point person. Cost: nothing.

  2. 02

    Five stores, two controls.

    Five stores with the oldest roofs in the hottest markets, treated in a day each. Two untouched stores of the same age and size. Treated before the cooling season, measured through it.

  3. 03

    Three bills, three measurements.

    Kilowatt hours and demand from utility interval data against the controls. The service log, twelve months before and during. Discharge temperature, head pressure and amp draw on every treated unit, before and 48 hours after. Space temperature in every pilot store so a hot day is data.

  4. 04

    Thirty days to the first review, one season to the number.

    Then the bottom quartile, about eighty stores, before the next summer. Then the fleet on Academy's own build and remodel cadence.
