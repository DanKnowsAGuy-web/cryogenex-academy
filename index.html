Every wasted kilowatt bills you three times. Recovered, it pays you back three times.

  • The meter A rooftop unit that has lost a quarter of its capacity to oil fouling still draws full power. It just runs longer to do the same job. That is the part of the bill you can see.
  • The demand charge In Texas, four summer afternoons set next year's transmission charges for every store on the ERCOT grid. A big box runs every rooftop unit flat out at exactly those hours. Units that recover capacity hit the peak lighter, and the credit lasts twelve months.
  • Equipment life Fouled units run hot and long, and they vent. Refrigerant leakage already shows up in Academy's own Scope 1 line. Restore the unit and it runs cooler, cycles less, and the replacement decision moves out on your terms.

One lever cuts all three.

None of this is a replacement program. It is a restoration of what the roof already has, applied in a day per store, with no shutdown and no modification, and the equipment warranty stays in place. Five levers, in the order we would pull them on an Academy roof.

  1. Restore what age took away

    A one time treatment of the refrigerant circuit that strips the insulating oil film off the coil walls. Up to 36 percent of lost efficiency recovered on units past their fifth year, about 10 percent on newer ones. This is the lead lever for a fleet of older Texas stores.

  2. Stop paying for wasted runtime

    Compressor intelligence for simple on and off units. The compressor stops once the coil is saturated and restarts only when it must. 15 to 30 percent of HVAC energy on the units it fits.

  3. Flatten the peaks

    Stage the rooftop units through the four summer peak windows so the store never hits the grid all at once. Demand and transmission savings on top of the energy savings.

  4. Condition the power

    Cleaner power for the compressors and the LED drivers. Fewer failures, longer life. A later phase.

  5. Replace on your terms

    When a unit is truly done, replace it with the best available, folded into the twenty to twenty five stores a year you already build and remodel, with the incentives captured at the right time.

The combined recovery

Combined, the first three levers recover 20 to 55 percent of the HVAC and cooling portion of a store's bill. We plan at the floor of that range and let the meter argue for the rest.

The pilot: prove it in five Houston stores first.

Katy is minutes from dozens of Houston stores. Your own people can walk the pilot.

  1. Send the folder.

    Twelve months of electric bills for the Texas stores, the rooftop unit nameplate list or photos, and one point person. No site visit needed to start. Cost: nothing.

  2. Pick five stores. We pick two more.

    Five Houston area stores with the oldest rooftop units, treated in a day each with no shutdown. Two untouched control stores of the same age and size. Measured under IPMVP through the summer peak window.

How it is priced

Rollout is priced as a fixed multiple of modeled first year savings, so the payback is written down before the first unit is treated. Texas utility programs can fund up to half of the project cost on verified projects. Typical payback across the Texas stores is 12 to 24 months. There is no subscription and nothing to maintain.

The bill review is free. The pilot is measured before anything scales. If the control stores and the treated stores cannot be told apart, nothing gets installed in store six and nothing is owed.
