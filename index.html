Bill one

Electricity. Led by the line no broker can touch.

Any broker can shop the price of a kilowatt hour. Nobody can shop the demand charge. It is set by your highest fifteen minutes of the month, when every rooftop unit is running flat out.

A fouled unit does not draw less. It runs longer and lands harder on that peak. Restore it and the peak drops, the hours drop, and the credit lasts twelve months. Solar thermal on a restored unit takes the rest of the way: at least 30 percent more.

On units past ten years, the recovery has measured 20 to 24 percent under IPMVP.

Electricity is 94 percent of Academy's measured footprint. Academy GHG report, 2021.

Bill two

Maintenance. Heat kills compressors.

Fouled coils run hot. Hot units fail, leak, and call for service in July.

Restore the heat transfer and the readings drop within 48 hours. We expect 15 to 25 percent fewer failures. That is a claim until the service log proves it. The pilot logs it.

Bill three

Replacement. $240,000 a roof. 322 roofs.

Twelve rooftop units a store. Fifteen years each. About $77 million of replacement across the fleet, arriving on failure, in summer.

A unit that runs cooler lasts longer. Three to five added years moves replacement onto your build schedule instead of the emergency line.

Mechanism

Why one treatment moves all three.

Compressor oil bakes onto the inside of the coil as an insulating film. Heat that should leave stays in the unit. One treatment strips the film and keeps the metal clean. No shutdown. No modification. Warranty intact. Solar thermal adds heat on the compressor's discharge side, so a restored unit does the same work with less electricity.
